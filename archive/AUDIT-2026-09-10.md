## 1. The wall-clock guard has a hard edge, and the failure sits just past it

`39dd52a` moved `MIN_SPAN_S` from an admission threshold into `settled()`,
where it applies only to a dwell with no resolvable transient:

```python
if r["amp_sigma"] < MIN_AMPLITUDE_SIGMA and r["span_s"] < MIN_SPAN_S:
    return False
```

The docstring says the clock "now applies exactly where the pole cannot be
trusted", and [commissioning](../docs/ltspm3/commissioning.md) now tells the
operator to "use whatever `--min-dwell` the plan calls for; the grader will say
per rung, in the journal, whether it believes the result". Both overclaim, and
the second one invites the failure.

Measured on the case the commit itself names — 145 K, tau 600 s, 0.76 K still
to go, the sensor's own 28.6 mK of noise — using the sweep tool's `fit_pole`
and `settled()` on 200 synthetic dwells per row:

| span | truly left | certified settled | graded |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 s | 0.70 K | 0 / 200 | 0 |
| 60 s | 0.69 K | **16 / 200** | 16, all "steady" |
| 62 s | 0.69 K | **16 / 200** | 16 |
| 90 s | 0.65 K | 3 / 200 | 3 |
| 120 s | 0.62 K | 0 / 200 | 0 |

The passing fits are exactly the ones the guard is for — amplitude 2 to 4
sigma, tau 10 to 20 s, a pole fitted to noise — and they pass because `span_s`
is 60.0 and the test is `< 60`. Their `T_inf` is wrong by 0.70 K. Run through
`dwell()`'s actual loop, first fit at `--min-dwell` and a refit every five
samples, **`--min-dwell 60` certified 10 of 100 rungs at 60 s, 0.69 K short**;
`--min-dwell 120` certified none before 600 s.

Two things follow. The failure is not new — the old code admitted a dwell at
60 s and applied only the rate bar, which a noise pole also passes — so
`39dd52a` is strictly better than what it replaced. But the same commit removed
the `--min-dwell` refusal and the docs now bless any value, and the value the
docs used to name as the floor is the one value at which the guard does
nothing. The `Options` default of 120 s is safe for this case; nothing says so.

In the archive the same class is 8 graded anchors: `amp_sigma` under 20 and
span under 600 s, every one above 145 K, every one "steady". Two of them at
147.1 K and 170.4 K fit at 0.7 and 1.4 sigma with tau at the fitter's floor and
are placed at their own sample mean; whether the cryostat was settled there is
not something the fit can know. They are inside the ODE's `t_max` of 192.6 K.

**What to do.** The guard is a wall clock standing in for a plant clock, and
the tool has the plant clock: `fitted_response.tau_s(T)` in `ltspm3`,
`link.tau_fast_s` in the sweep. A dwell with no resolvable transient should
have to run some multiple of the plant's tau at its own temperature, not 60 s.
`analysis/steps.py` has no plant model and should not import one, so there the
honest option is finding 2's: refuse to believe a pole whose tau sits at a
search bound. Either way, the commissioning sentence should say 120 s until the
grader can back the promise.

## 2. Nothing notices when the fitter hits its own bound

`fit_pole` searches tau on `[2 × cadence, 20 × span]`. A result *at* either end
is the search saying "outside what I can see", and both `grade()`s treat it as
a measurement. From the fresh `steps.csv`:

**At the floor, tau = 4.0 s exactly, 48 graded anchors, 8 of them "tau"** — at
5.41, 5.41, 5.94, 6.57, 7.26, 8.05, 19.76 and 25.10 K. The plant's tau there,
from the shipped table, is under 0.1 s below 8 K, 1.1 s at 20 K and 3.4 s at
25 K. So the eight are two samples' worth of cadence presented as time
constants, and `fit_ode.load_taus` takes every `"tau"` row with no floor test,
at `TAU_SIGMA_FACTOR = 1.5` in log tau. A 4 s "measurement" of a 0.1 s plant is
a 9 sigma residual in that objective, pulling C(T) at exactly the cold end the
refit plan says has too little leverage. The other 40 floor-pinned rows are
"steady" and are fine: the steady state of a fast relaxation is real even when
its tau is not resolvable.

**At the ceiling, tau = 20 × span exactly, 7 graded anchors, all "steady",
reach 0.05:**

| T_inf | span | fitted tau | settle | era |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.84 K | 38 s | 751 s | 0.05 K | recorder |
| 5.14 K | 78 s | 1560 s | −0.15 K | recorder |
| 99.42 K | 840 s | 16800 s | −0.18 K | prepython |
| 118.09 K | 9598 s | 191959 s | −0.16 K | postcal |
| 141.99 K | 35440 s | 708796 s | −0.76 K | prepython |
| 146.00 K | 59910 s | 1198196 s | **−1.36 K** | prepython |
| 170.64 K | 232 s | 4640 s | −0.53 K | recorder |

They pass via the rate bar: with tau pinned at 20 × span the fitted end rate is
`171 × amp / span` K/h, which is under 0.5 for any amplitude below `span / 342`
K — so a 232 s dwell passes on any transient under 0.7 K whatever the plant is
doing. The `T_inf` is then the extrapolation of a bound, not of the data: the
146.00 K row is a 16.6 h hold placed 1.36 K beyond where it ended, on a tau of
14 days, in a cooldown known to drift 0.167 K/day. That is the campaign drift
being read as a relaxation, which is trap T3 of [REFIT_PLAN.md](../REFIT_PLAN.md)
arriving through the grader. The anchor's error bar (`2 × settle`, so 2.7 K,
in quadrature with 3.0) keeps its weight low. It should not be an anchor.

Thirteen in-band anchors have reach under 1.0; the seven above are the ones the
fitter gave up on outright.

**What to do.** `fit_pole` returns tau and knows its bounds. Return whether tau
is pinned — or have `grade()` test `tau_s <= 1.05 × floor` and
`tau_s >= 0.95 × 20 × span` — and refuse `"tau"` for a floor pin, refuse any
grade for a floor pin whose amplitude is under `MIN_AMPLITUDE_SIGMA` (that is
a noise pole, and it is the two 147 K and 170 K rows above), and refuse any
grade for a ceiling pin. Mirror it in `ltspm3/tools/sweep.py`, where the same
bounds apply and the same rate bar passes the same ceiling pin, and add the new
constant to `test_the_mirrored_grader_constants_still_match_analysis_steps`.
Then `curate.py --propose` shows the verdict changes as a diff, which is what
it is for.

## 3. One test writes a rehearsal journal into the working directory

`test_a_short_dwell_with_a_real_transient_in_it_is_allowed` calls
`S.main([... "--simulate"])` with no `--journal`. The simulator's status carries
no recorder path, so `default_journal_path` falls back to `"."`, and every test
run leaves a `sweep-<stamp>.csv` in whatever directory pytest was started from.
Confirmed: `sweep-20260910-114225.csv` appeared in this worktree's root during
the baseline run. The blanket `*.csv` in `.gitignore` hides it, so the previous
audit's finding 6 — three stray rehearsal journals in the repo root — is now
self-renewing. It also breaks the house rule that a test must not depend on the
working directory, in the write direction.

**What to do.** Pass `"--journal", str(tmp_path / "rehearsal.csv")`. One line.
The other `main()` test uses `--plan-only` and writes nothing.

## 4. Every clock in the analysis path is naive local time

The recorder stamps each row with `datetime.fromtimestamp(t_wall)`, naive and
local. `fit_table._stamp`, `segments._stamp` and the viewer's `CsvTail` all
parse it back with `fromisoformat(...).timestamp()`. On **2026-11-01 at 02:00**
the Pacific clock falls back and 01:00–02:00 happens twice; the parse maps both
hours onto the first, so `epoch` runs backwards by an hour and then catches up.

Nothing detects it. `fit_table`'s segment test is `t - prev > gap_limit`, which
a negative step does not trip, so `t_s` goes non-monotonic inside one segment;
`Table.slice` is `searchsorted` on an array that is no longer sorted and
selects wrong rows without an error; `dwells()` fits a pole to a time axis with
a fold in it. The archive is clean today — every table checked strictly
monotonic — because the cooldown began in July. Cooldown 10 is still running,
and the third table is appended to.

**What to do.** The recorder already writes a monotonic relative clock, `Time`,
beside the stamp. `fit_table` should take `t_s` from it and use the stamp only
to place the file's origin; that fixes the archive at its source and costs no
format change. `segments.read_table` can then assert monotonic `epoch` and
refuse otherwise, which is cheap and turns a silent fold into a message.
Writing the UTC offset into the stamp is the right long-term answer and a
format change that MATLAB and the viewer both parse, so not in passing.

## What is right, and worth not breaking

- **`curate.py --propose` is a regression test and it passes.** The dataset is
  a committed file, a finder change arrives as a diff, and re-running it on a
  clean tree reproduces every row. This is the right shape, and finding 2's
  fix will show up in it as a list of verdict changes rather than as a
  different number of anchors nobody can account for.
- **The era column replaced a filename test with a raise.** Five places keyed
  on `source.startswith("fit_cd10")` are now one map with no default, and an
  anchor table from before the column existed is refused with a sentence
  saying why. The silent failure that description names was the real one.
- **Masks are an input to the proposal, not a filter on it.** One mechanism
  for "do not use this", the dwell is cut at the mask's edge, and `--propose`
  still reports no diff. The 09-09 power transient is in the manifest as a
  window with a paragraph on it, which is where a judgement belongs.
- **The cache key now covers every constant the objective reads**, found
  because a knob looked dead. That is the second time this repository has
  caught that failure mode, and the second time it wrote it down.
- **The heater is not the exposure.** Every finding here changes which
  numbers reach the refit. The sweep tool's abort paths still hold the output;
  `status.json` still reports `control: null`; nothing here can move a heater.
