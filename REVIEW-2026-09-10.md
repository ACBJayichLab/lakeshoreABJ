## 1. Option 4 — the grader's guard now runs on a measured plant clock

**Problem.** `analysis/steps.py` graded a dwell "settled" using `reach`,
`remainder_K` and `end_rate_k_per_h`, all computed **from the fitted time
constant**. When that time constant is meaningless — the pole was fitted to
noise, or it pinned at the top of the search where an exponential degenerates
to a straight line — all three are meaningless too, and they fail
*optimistically*. The guard against that was a 60 s wall clock, on a plant
whose τ spans 0.1 s to 850 s. It was wrong at both ends: it had thrown away 46 s
dwells that ran 11.5 time constants, and it was admitting 200 s dwells that ran
a third of one.

**Why it could not be fixed before.** The right test is "did this dwell run
several of the *plant's* time constants", and invariant 1 forbids `analysis/`
importing a plant model. The live sweep tool got this fix first (`c58b264`)
because it has the plan's τ predictions in the room.

**What changed.** Invariant 1 forbids `analysis/` *importing* a plant model; it
does not forbid it **measuring** one. `steps.plant_clock` interpolates τ(T)
log-log through the dwells graded `tau`, and `steps.long_enough` asks
`MIN_REACH × τ(T)`. No new constant.

### Three bugs found and fixed in passing

## 3. Step 5 — `analysis/drift.py`, the gate

**Finding: the campaign drift is a power, not a temperature.**

A drift quoted in K/day is not a property of the cryostat — it is the
underlying change times the *local gain*, which runs 2 to 14 K/% across the
band. Three independent output bands, de-overlapped so no anchor is counted
twice:

| band % | n | days | T range | K/% | K/W | K/day | **mW/day** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 52.0–53.5 | 9 | 50.0 | 27.2–32.0 K | 2.04 | 118.7 | 0.0334 | **0.281** |
| 62.9–64.4 | 9 | 48.5 | 98.4–118.6 K | 11.52 | 555.1 | 0.1862 | **0.335** |
| 65.2–66.7 | 30 | 25.4 | 129.2–148.9 K | 13.67 | 634.4 | 0.1413 | **0.223** |

**K/day spans 5.6×; mW/day agrees to ±25 %.** So step 8's campaign ramp must
enter at the heater's node in watts, as the existing within-record term already
does. A temperature offset would be wrong at both ends by construction.

**Three routes, one number.** Median **+0.281 mW/day** here; REFIT_PLAN §2.3
independently measured **+0.27 mW/day** in a band this table does not reuse;
and the 12/4 fit's own per-era `group_w` offset is **4.60 mW** across the ~20
days between the two eras' centroids, against 0.281 × 20 = 5.6 mW.

**The gain column is the independent check** REFIT_PLAN §2.3 specifies, and it
passes: 11.5 K/% at 98–119 K and 13.7 at 129–149 K, against the fit's 13–15.

**Coverage decides the knot count and says affine.** A knot interval whose
anchors do not span temperature cannot separate "the cryostat got warmer" from
"Λ is lower here" — both lift every anchor in the interval equally. Test: ≥ 5
anchors in each of two clumps ≥ 3× apart in T, reported at the most *balanced*
passing split.

```
affine     days  0.0-55.3   n=136   4.8-247.6 K   EARNED  (68 near 17 K, 68 near 135 K)
3 knots    days  0.0-27.7   n= 12  22.5-247.6 K   NO      no split 3x apart, 5 either side
           days 27.7-55.3   n=124   4.8-192.4 K   ok      (62 near 15 K, 62 near 133 K)
```

A bare max/min ratio passes the failing interval at 11.0 and separates nothing,
which is why the test is not a ratio.

**Decision:** the regression is deliberately **unweighted**. The anchors' bars
are dominated by `ANCHOR_SIGMA_K`, which is 3.0 K for `prepython` and 1.0 for
the rest — an era *label*, not a measurement — so weighting by them would
down-weight exactly the old half of the campaign that carries the date
leverage, and the drift would come back small for a reason that has nothing to
do with the cryostat. That is trap T3 arriving early.

```bash
$V analysis/drift.py
$V analysis/drift.py --knots 3
```

## 4. Step 6 — seed Λ and C from the anchors

`fit_ode.py --seed-only` prints Λ and C with **no `least_squares` and no ODE
integrated anywhere**: Λ by direct inversion of `Λ(T_s) − Λ(T_c) = Q` at every
settled dwell (T_c folded in by iterating; it converges in two passes because Λ
at 5–7 K is a percent of Q), C from `τ·Λ′` at every measured relaxation.

**It agrees with the fit to a few percent, from two methods sharing no
machinery:**

| | model-free | converged fit |
|---|---|---|
| C(137 K) | 1.006 J/K = **4.960 g** of the Debye mix | **4.806 g** |
| τ(137 K) | ~610 s | 571 s |
| local resistance at 130 K | 620 K/W | 639 K/W between the two September holds |

### The finding that matters: the seed was choosing the local minimum

Production 20/4, run to convergence. `cost` is `0.5·Σr²` — what
`least_squares` actually minimises — and it is on the row because `rms_k` and
`anchor_k` are two *weighted parts* of it and here they move in opposite
directions:

| preset | seed | cap | **cost** | `rms_k` | `anchor_k` | `mass_g` | τ(137) | `nfev` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12/4 | power law | 300 | 1366.46 | 0.2025 | 1.5426 | 4.7877 | 567.1 | 300 **cut** |
| 12/4 | power law | 1500 | 1364.96 | 0.2081 | 1.5428 | 4.7866 | 567.3 | 539 ✓ |
| 12/4 | **measured** | 300 | **1252.25** | 0.1869 | 1.5481 | 4.7705 | 559.4 | **128** ✓ |
| 12/4 | **measured** | 1500 | **1252.25** | 0.1869 | 1.5481 | 4.7705 | 559.4 | **128** ✓ |
| 20/4 | power law | 300 | 1164.29 | 0.1579 | 1.5237 | 4.7892 | 566.5 | 300 **cut** |
| 20/4 | power law | 1500 | 1163.02 | 0.1585 | 1.5244 | 4.7887 | 566.6 | 1157 ✓ |
| 20/4 | **measured** | 300 | **994.64** | 0.1499 | 1.5479 | 4.8059 | 571.3 | **149** ✓ |
| 20/4 | **measured** | 1500 | **994.64** | 0.1499 | 1.5479 | 4.8059 | 571.3 | **149** ✓ |

`cost` is `0.5·Σr²` — what `least_squares` actually minimises — and it is on the
row because `rms_k` and `anchor_k` are two *weighted parts* of it and they move
in opposite directions here.

**The two seeds converge to different optima.** The measured one is **8.3 %
better at 12 knots and 14.5 % at 20**, and gets there **4.2× and 7.8× faster**
(128 vs 539, 149 vs 1157). The objective is multi-modal and the power-law seed
had been landing in the worse basin for the whole campaign, so every production
number this repository has quoted comes from there.

**`MAX_NFEV` 300 → 1500.** 300 was a truncation: neither seed had converged
there. With the gauge search gone the shipped path no longer needs the
headroom — it converges in 128–149 — so 1500 is there so that a comparison
*against* the old seed is between two converged fits rather than between a fit
and a truncation, and it costs nothing on rungs that stop earlier.

Note the better optimum has a **higher** `anchor_k` with a lower `rms_k`. That
is REFIT_PLAN §1's tension — the model as posed cannot satisfy the trajectory
and the holds at once — appearing in the shape of the basin rather than in a
weight study.

### Two things I got wrong and then measured

**A single Debye magnitude for C was not good enough.** Fitting one factor to
the mix reconstructed τ(137 K) as **804 s where the τ anchors there say 607** —
32 % out, because a median ratio over 25–192 K lands its error wherever C
departs from Debye most, and that is not where you want it. C's *shape* now
comes from the τ anchors over 28.4–192.4 K and falls back to Debye outside,
scaled to match at the join. Below 25 K there is no τ to read C from, C falls
three orders of magnitude, and the shape prior holds it to that mix anyway.

**The gauge search was worse than useless, and this one was caught only while
writing this document.** Λ's level is a null direction of the data (trap T1)
*and* of the roughness penalty, since the penalty acts on `log(dΛ/dT)` and
`d(Λ+c)/dT = dΛ/dT`. My first `_gauge_level` therefore measured a quantity
invariant to its own argument and returned the top of its grid (9.68 W against
a curve spanning 0.97). My second searched the genuine residue — the
parameterisation interpolates **log Λ** between knots, so a constant moves the
curve *between* them — through the real `LogLog`. That was defensible.
Measuring it killed it:

| gauge | cost | `nfev` |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **994.64** | **149** |
| 0.0175 — the search's answer at 20 knots | 994.64 | 566 |
| 2.4121 — its answer at 9 knots, railed at 3× the span | **1163.02** | 561 |

Zero reaches the same optimum **3.8× faster**, and the 9-knot answer drops the
fit into the *worse* basin — the one the power-law seed was stuck in — which
would have destroyed step 6's entire benefit at low knot counts, and the ladder
runs from 3 knots up. `_gauge_level` is **deleted**; Λ is seeded on the anchors'
own `Λ(T_c) = 0`. The table is kept in `measured_lambda`'s docstring so nobody
adds it back.
