## 1. The sweep mirrors the grader's constants but not its admission thresholds

**This one has already cost data.** `ltspm3/tools/sweep.py:96` explains that the
grader constants are duplicated from `analysis/steps.py` on purpose, and that

> if they ever diverge the symptom is a sweep whose points the fitter throws
> away -- which the journal's `grade` column reports on the spot rather than a
> week later.

The two thresholds that actually threw points away are the ones not mirrored.
`analysis/steps.py:124` admits a dwell only at `min_span_s=60.0, min_n=15`, and
that test runs **upstream of `grade()`** — so the journal cannot report it. The
dwell is discarded before it is ever graded.

Reproduced against the simulator, using the three rungs the real run lost:

```
$ python -m ltspm3.tools.sweep --percents 45.31,47.69,49.82 --min-dwell 45 --simulate
  #      u %  T pred     T_inf  settle   tau s  reach  span s  grade
  1   45.310            20.532  -0.112       4   11.5      46  steady
  2   47.690            22.929   0.016       4   11.5      46  steady
  3   49.820            25.450   0.006       4   11.5      46  steady

3/3 dwells graded
```

Three 46 s dwells; the journal says 3/3 graded; `steps.py` keeps none. That is
exactly what happened on 2026-09-05 with `--min-dwell 45`, written up in
[`HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md) and in
[commissioning](docs/ltspm3/commissioning.md). The lesson landed there as
operator discipline — *use `--min-dwell 75`* — but not in the tool, so the next
person with a reason to shorten a dwell repeats it and is told they succeeded.

**What to do.** Mirror `MIN_SPAN_S = 60.0` and `MIN_N = 15` beside the other
four constants, clamp `--min-dwell` up to 60 or refuse below it, and change
`dwell()`'s break gate at `sweep.py:869` from `len(samples) >= 8` to
`>= MIN_N`. The `min_n` half is latent at the live 2 s cadence — 60 s is 30
samples — and bites above 4 s.

## 2. The shipped fitted table is known 4.5 K low, and nothing in `ltspm3/` says so

The programmed ladder measured the steady state **low by up to 4.5 K across
40–98 K, 25 times the fit's own 0.168 K residual**, while τ came back within 3%
from 77 K to 114 K. The refit has not been run.

That is recorded in [`HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md), in
[thermal response](docs/ltspm3/thermal-response.md), in
[commissioning](docs/ltspm3/commissioning.md), in `analysis/_data.py:59` and in
`analysis/README.md`. It is recorded nowhere in the package that ships the
table:

- `ltspm3/_fitted_table.py`'s provenance header dates and describes the fit and
  does not say it has since been contradicted;
- `ltspm3/fitted_response.py`'s module docstring presents the curve as the
  correction to `sim_response`, which it is, without saying it is itself
  superseded;
- `ltspm3/tools/sweep.py`'s docstring table quotes the steady-state predictions
  as current — and they *are* faithfully what the code computes, which is the
  problem.

`--simulate` and `analysis/plan_sweep.py` are both driven by it, so a rehearsal
or a re-plan run next month inherits the error silently.
[`HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md) goes stale by design; invariant 9 says the measured
number wins. Until the refit runs, a paragraph in the generated header and a
line in `fitted_response.py` is the cheap half — and the refit command sequence
is already written down in the handoff.

Related, in the same header: the fit reports **0.168 K rms and 11.03 K max**
residual with no comment on the 65× ratio. Whichever points carry that maximum
are worth a sentence.

## 3. Time constants are fitted off the recorder's wall clock

`RecorderLink.sample()` (`sweep.py:505`) takes each sample's `t_s` from
`status["t_wall"]`, and `status.json` publishes no monotonic clock
(`lschart/ipc/status.py:430`). Every dwell's `span_s`, `tau_s` and
`end_rate_k_per_h` is computed from those stamps.

So a clock step — an NTP correction on a Windows machine that has been up for
days — lands inside a dwell as a jump in the independent variable and corrupts
the fit, at a moment when the fit is the stop rule. The recorder's own CSV
avoids precisely this by writing `t_mono - t0` for its relative clock, which is
the project's stated convention: `time.monotonic()` for every interval,
`time.time()` only for the absolute stamp.

**What to do.** Stamp each deduplicated sample with the client's own
`time.monotonic()`. The dedup on the recorder's `cycle` counter already
guarantees exactly one stamp per acquisition, and sub-0.5 s poll latency against
a 2 s cadence is nothing beside a 36 s τ at the bottom of the band that matters.
Adding `t_mono` to the status schema is the other route and costs a schema
version.

## 4. `main()` is untested, including the last two gates before a real heater

`tests_ltspm3/test_sweep_tool.py` is 38 tests and good ones: the pole fitter
against a known relaxation, the striding, every preflight refusal, the
ack timeout, the coldplate ceiling, the journal on disk before a run that dies
halfway. `main()` has none.

Two of the paths it alone contains are the ones where being wrong is expensive:

- the interactive **"Type yes to start"** confirmation before the first write;
- `--on-abort off` → `set_output_panic()` → the `heaters_off` panic, which cuts
  *every* writable heater on the recorder — on LTSPM3 that includes the 336's,
  holding THE CHONKE.

`set_output_panic` is exercised only through the test file's own stub
(`test_sweep_tool.py:77`), never through a `RecorderLink`.

## 5. `ipc.sources` is the one gate the heater config does not mention

Invariant 3 counts `ipc.sources` among the seven write interlocks. Empty means
open (`lschart/config.py:320`, deliberately — it is what every pre-existing
config means), so on `config-ltspm3-heater.yaml`, the one config that can move
the sample heater, any process able to write into `data/commands` may.

That file's "WHAT THIS FILE TURNS OFF, AND WHAT IT LEAVES ON" block enumerates
the other six interlocks, in detail, and does not mention this one.
`lschart check` does not print it either. There are now three named clients —
`lschart-cli/<pid>`, MATLAB, and `ltspm3-sweep/<pid>` — so naming them costs one
block, and the config's own convention is to say what is open and why.

## What is right, and worth not breaking

- **`ltspm3/control/filters.py` was corrected by docstring only.** Measurement
  contradicted it — 0.18× promised against 0.41×/0.73× delivered — in a commit
  that had every excuse to change behaviour, and it changed none. Invariant 9
  and the standing instruction to leave `control/` alone both held under
  pressure.
- **`recalibrate.py` refuses to invert a railed reading**, for the right reason,
  and the reason is in the module docstring: the wrong Coldplate curve rails at
  330.32 K and inverting the clamp yields 292.6 K, which is indistinguishable
  from a plausible room temperature. A blank is honest.
- **`noisespec.py` refuses a τ below the record's own cadence** rather than
  returning a plausible number, and `_moving_average` returns `None` rather than
  let `np.convolve(mode="same")` report the kernel length as a window.
- **The sweep holds on abort.** Every abort path leaves the output exactly where
  it is; `heaters_off` is opt-in and the docstring says plainly why it is the
  wrong choice on this cryostat.
