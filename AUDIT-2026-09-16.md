## 1. The bench reproduces the 13:35 walk-down, and nothing runs the armed configuration

HANDOFF item D says three attempts could not reproduce the first arm on the
harness. They could not because the plant was started *at the model's answer*:
`FittedHarness` sets `plant.pct = plant.percent_for(kelvin)`, which is the
nominal curve, so a plant with `delivered_frac < 1` begins **out of
equilibrium and falling**, and the loop's first minutes are spent catching a
disturbance that has nothing to do with feedforward. Feedforward on and off
then give bit-identical traces, because the feedforward term is referenced at
arming and a fixed setpoint never moves it.

The cryostat on the 16th was not in that state. It had sat for hours on a
heater delivering 0.336 % less than the model claims, **settled**, 1.4 K
below the model's answer for its output. Rehearse that and the walk-down is
there:

```python
# tests_ltspm3, virtual clock.  delivered_frac = 1 - 0.00336, the measured shift.
h = FittedHarness(kelvin=118.3, delivered_frac=0.99664, sup_cfg=sup, ff_cfg=ff)
st = h.step(3600)          # 2 h OPEN LOOP: settle on the weak heater -> 116.88 K
h.sup.arm(st.filtered_k)   # arm where it is, as the operator did
```

| feedforward | authority | band at +5 min | ΔT at 5 min | ΔT at 40 min | output at 40 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| on | 0.1 | 63.776–63.976 | **−0.445 K** | −0.171 K | 63.970 (railed at the ceiling) |
| on | 0.25 | 63.626–64.126 | **−0.445 K** | −0.017 K | 63.980 |
| off | 0.1 | 63.860–64.060 | +0.001 K | 0.000 K | 63.980 |
| off | 0.25 | 63.710–64.210 | +0.001 K | 0.000 K | 63.980 |

Two mechanisms, and the trace separates them. The **dip** (63.984 → 63.930 in
the first minutes, 0.45 K) is the feedforward path at arming and happens at
either width. The **failure to recover** at 0.1 is the band: with feedforward
on the centre is the model's stale 63.876, so the ceiling lands at 63.976,
*below* the 63.984 the plant actually needs, and the loop sits railed 0.17 K
low for as long as you care to run it. That is the "walked to 63.92 and stayed
there" of the real trace. With feedforward off neither mechanism exists.

So HANDOFF section 4's diagnosis is right, D is closable in an afternoon, and
the recipe is the thing the harness was missing: **a plant that has settled on
a wrong heater, not one that starts wrong.** `FittedHarness` should grow a
`settled=True` option (or `equilibrate()`), because a cryostat is never handed
to the loop mid-transient in practice.

The second half of this finding is why it is number one. `bench_plant.py` now
pins `BENCH_AUTHORITY_PCT = 1.0` and `BENCH_FEEDFORWARD = True`, and the
harness has never read `tuning` from the file at all — `Harness` passes no
`tuning_config`, so `TuningConfig()` defaults to enabled. The three switches
the cryostat runs on today are therefore **feedforward off, tuning off, band
0.25**, and the three the bench grades are **on, on, 1.0**. The only test with
the feedforward disabled is a unit test on `PID._ff`
(`test_regime.py:78`). 497 tests passing "unchanged" when `93c6f9c` flipped
the file is presented as proof that fix B works. It is also proof that no test
noticed. Findings 2 and 3 are what they would have noticed.

**Suggest:** a `tests_ltspm3/test_stage_4a.py` that builds `FittedHarness`
from the *file's* stage switches (a `stage="file"` argument beside the
envelope default) and pins four things: the table above; that a hold at
118 K stays within `warn_error_k`; the fault ramp-down duration (finding 2);
the output rate at a 3 K setpoint move (finding 3). Then HANDOFF D becomes a
row in `plans/pid-4-commissioning.md` instead of a gap.

## 2. With feedforward off the fault ramp-down descends at 0.20 %/min

`_rampdown_target` (supervisor.py, from about line 1836):

```python
if self._rampdown_t0 is None:
    self._rampdown_from_k = (self.filter.value if self.filter.primed
                             else (self.feedforward.kelvin_for(current)
                                   if self.feedforward.enabled else None))
...
if self._rampdown_from_k is not None and self.feedforward.enabled:
    ...  # the one rate, in kelvin, through the inverse curve
else:
    # **No curve at all** -- a cryostat with no fitted response, which
    # is the only way to get here now ...
    proposed = current - self._rate_pct_per_min(None) * (dt / 60.0)
```

The armed config has `feedforward.enabled: false`, so every fault ramp-down on
this cryostat today takes the `else` branch, and the comment's "only way to
get here" is false. `_rate_pct_per_min(None)` is the floor,
`min_rate_pct_per_min: 0.20`. From 63.99 % that is **320 minutes**. The
supervisor's own comment two screens up quotes the number it replaced — "63 %
to zero in over five hours, measured, against the 23 minutes the documents
promise" — and `docs/ltspm3/control.md` still promises the one rate for "a
sweep, the post-fault approach and the fault ramp-down". `_rampdown_step_pct`
has the same gate and the same fallback.

Is five hours dangerous? Rule 1 says the fault response is freeze and slow
ramp-down, and slower is the safe side of that rule. But it is not the
behaviour anyone has reasoned about, tested, or documented for 4a, and a
sensor fault at 118 K now means a heater at 64 % for the rest of the
afternoon on a cryostat whose thermometer is not trusted. `safety.md`'s rule 3
argument (the fault may *be* the sensor, so the descent needs no sensor) was
made for a descent that walks the curve; the floor-rate descent is blind to
temperature entirely.

The root cause is a conflated switch. `feedforward.enabled` answers "should
the loop *trust the model's level* as a control term" — which is what 4a
correctly says no to — and the ramp-down asks "is there a *curve* to walk
down", which is a different question with a different answer. The shape of
the curve did not expire on the 16th; only its level did, and a descent at
5 K/min on a curve 1.4 K off is still a descent at about 5 K/min.

**Suggest:** give the supervisor one `model` seam (the `Feedforward` object
already wraps `_M`) and gate the ramp-down, `_rampdown_step_pct` and finding
3's rate conversion on *whether a curve exists*, not on whether the
feedforward term is enabled. Until then, a line in `plans/pid-4-commissioning.md`
under "No feedforward is load-bearing" saying what it costs, and a bench test
that measures the descent duration under the file's switches. This is a
`control/` change: rule 1 and rule 3, one commit, with the virtual-clock test
first.

## 3. With tuning off the output rate limiter is on the floor, and `set_setpoint` does not know

`_rate_pct_per_min` returns `min_rate_pct_per_min` whenever
`not self.tuner.enabled`. At 118 K and ~13.2 K/%, 0.20 %/min is **2.6 K/min**,
not the 5 the file's `ramp.max_rate_k_per_min` names; at 30 K it would be
0.6 K/min. `ramp_lead_pct` returns 0 for the same reason, so the band does not
widen during a ramp (harmless while it is fixed anyway).

Meanwhile `set_setpoint` still turns a step larger than `warn_error_k` into a
ramp at `max_rate_k_per_min`, in kelvin, through the smoother. The output
limiter cannot follow that ramp, so the error grows during the move — for a
3 K move, to roughly 1.4 K at the end of the 36 s ramp, against an output that
needs 68 s to travel its 0.23 % — and `warn_error_k` is suspended only while
the setpoint is moving. It may fire in the tail. Not a hazard, but a loop that
is told 5 K/min and delivers 2.6 with a warning is a loop somebody will
"tune" without knowing why. Same conflation as finding 2: `tuner.enabled`
means "reschedule the gains", and the conversion from kelvin to percent needs
the curve, not the scheduler.

**Suggest:** same seam as finding 2. Meanwhile record the effective rate at
4a in the commissioning plan, because 4d's 10 K sweep is written against
5 K/min.

## 4. `arm` while armed is fixed at the button, not at the supervisor

`219422c` greys the viewer's arm button while `mode == "pid"` because
`arm()` runs `set_setpoint(x, ramp=False)` before a `set_mode` that no-ops.
Correct diagnosis, wrong layer. `IPCService._do_arm` → `Application.arm` →
`HeaterSupervisor.arm` is unchanged, so `send arm` from the CLI or MATLAB on a
tracking loop still steps the setpoint to the present filtered temperature and
resets `_pending_approach` and the smoother. Rule 8 bounds the step to
`warn_error_k` (anything larger becomes a ramp), so the consequence is a
sub-kelvin step and a dumped trajectory rather than a hazard — but the button
now says one thing and the command spool does another, and MATLAB has only the
spool.

**Suggest:** `arm()` refuses (or returns "already armed, setpoint unchanged")
when `self.mode is LoopMode.PID`, with a harness test that arms twice and
asserts the setpoint and the smoother are untouched. The button gating stays
as a courtesy. `control/`, one commit, rule 8.

## 5. The viewer's arm button can stick disabled

`_update_gate_notes` says it "only ever DISABLES" the arm button and leaves
re-enabling to the pending-command logic. That logic is `_update_pending`,
which re-enables `_buttons()` only when a command *the viewer itself queued*
is acknowledged or times out. Nothing else calls `setEnabled(True)` on it
(`window.py:2464` disables; `2577` is the only enable).

So: viewer open, loop armed, arm button grey. Jeff types `send hold` in a
terminal, or MATLAB does. Mode goes `off`, `software_loop_owns_output()` goes
False, and the arm button stays grey until the viewer happens to queue and
acknowledge some other command. The way back from a hold — which is what the
button is for — is unavailable exactly after a hold that did not come from the
viewer.

The analog spin and button are set unconditionally each tick
(`setEnabled(analog_ok)`), so they do not have this problem. The arm button
should be treated the same way: `setEnabled(not loop_owns and self._pending is None)`,
with `_update_pending` still free to lock everything while a command is in
flight. `tests/test_gui_window.py` has one `arm_button.click()` test and none
for the gating; a status with `mode: pid` then `mode: off` across two
refreshes would pin both directions.

## 6. `send hold` refuses on a stale status file

`cmd_send` refuses to queue anything if `status.json` is older than
`max(3 × interval_s, 5 s)` = 6 s, or says `running: false`. Sensible for a
setpoint. For the panic kinds it is the wrong default, and this session
observed both of the conditions that produce a stale-but-alive recorder:

* HANDOFF item F: `PermissionError: [WinError 5]` on the status `os.replace`,
  because a reader holds the file at the instant of the rename. Two
  consecutive misses put the file past 6 s.
* HANDOFF item H: the first cycle after both VISA resources open overruns by
  2.8 s, every time.

A recorder that is alive but momentarily not writing status is the one you
most want `hold` to reach. The spool's TTL already bounds the cost of queueing
into a dead recorder (30 s, then the command is refused as expired), and a
`hold` that is never read costs nothing. The recorder side already exempts the
panic kinds from the source policy and the power gates; the CLI should exempt
them from the liveness guard too, print the warning, and queue anyway. Also
`status` accepts `--file` and `send` does not, so the two cannot be pointed at
the same alternative path.

On F itself: the writer already handles it (edge-logged, counted, tmp file
unlinked), and the suggested fix — readers opening with share-delete semantics
— is not available from `open()` in Python. The practical alternative is one
retry after a few milliseconds inside `atomic_write_json`, which turns a
sharing violation into a late write instead of a missed one.

## 7. The record is already behind the cryostat

Small, but the whole point of the handoff is that a reader who sees only it
has the picture.

* **HANDOFF.md "state at session end"**: "the running loop is still on 0.1".
  The status file at 15:48 shows rails 63.71–64.21 and a setpoint of 117.11 K
  with 382 cycles since start, i.e. restarted at about 15:36 on the 0.25
  band. Whoever restarted it may also be the parallel session; either way the
  handoff should not carry a live number that a `status` call answers better.
* **`config-ltspm3-armed.yaml` header**: "Nothing has been armed with it. The
  loop has never closed on this cryostat" and "Phase 3's bench loads its
  LIMITS from here". Both false since `219422c`. The stage-switch caveat lives
  only in the `authority_pct` comment.
* **`operating_point_pct: 63.960 # fitted: 63.960% settles at 118.3 K`**: on
  the cryostat as wired today it settles about 116.7 K, and with feedforward
  off this number *is* the band centre. The plan says so; the field's own
  comment should.
* **"±3.3 K, about 114.7–121.3 K on the model"** (config and HANDOFF 7): the
  band is in percent and the cryostat is 0.107 % of output away from the
  model. From the present hold — 63.99 % at 117.07 K — the rails are
  −0.28 %/+0.22 %, about **−3.7 K / +2.9 K**, so 113.4 to 120.0 K. A setpoint
  past the top rails the demand at the ceiling with a sub-5 K error, which is
  a standing `warn_error_k` WARNING, not a fault; worth saying, because the
  config comment two fields down reads as though railing at the ceiling is a
  fault regardless.
* **supervisor.py ramp-down `else` comment**: "the only way to get here now"
  — finding 2.
* **`cmd_check`** prints `operating_point_pct ± authority_pct` as the
  authority band. Correct today because the centre is fixed; wrong from 4c on,
  when the band follows the setpoint. It should say which it is printing, or
  ask the supervisor.
* **transport.py**: "Root DEBUG on its own no longer means 'and the bus too'"
  is true through the CLI. `_quieten_vendor_logging` itself still returns
  early when the root is at DEBUG, and never touches `pyvisa`, so a transport
  built outside the CLI at root DEBUG gets the full flood. Say "via the CLI",
  or make the backstop match.

## 9. Smaller

* **Allan asymmetry** (HANDOFF 6). The one-hour window's 6.83 mK at τ = 264 s
  (edf ≈ 13) is quoted as evidence the loop is quieter than open loop, while
  the same window's NOT MET at τ = 480 and 874 s is dismissed for low edf. The
  first claim needs its confidence interval before it is repeated, and the
  open-loop 7.38 mK it is compared to was measured on a different day on a
  different-length window. "Consistent with no added noise" is what the hour
  supports.
* **`software_loop_owns_output()` returns False for `manual`**, but
  `LoopMode.MANUAL` writes `manual_pct` (clamped, rate-limited) every cycle
  and would overwrite a viewer command just as `pid` does. Not reachable from
  the spool today (`hold` goes to `off`), so a note rather than a fix; if a
  `manual` command ever appears, `mode != "off"` is the ownership test.
* **`LS218._confirm`** sleeps `pause_s` after the last failed attempt before
  raising. Pre-existing; 100 ms.
* **W1's evidence** is three steps of 0.021–0.057 %. Invariant 5 says the
  check is undecidable below `readback_tol_pct` and every dither write is
  below it; W1 correctly answers the question it asked (is 100 ms enough when
  the step is decidable). The plan should keep saying that the armed loop's
  readback is *silent by arithmetic* on hold writes, so a future reader does
  not take "W1 MET" as "readback verifies the hold".
